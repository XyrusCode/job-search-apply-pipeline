# Cover-letter voice notes (reusable base)

Principles for keeping generated letters readable and human. Tune these against your own sent letters:
if this note and your real letters disagree, follow the real letters and update this note.

## The skeleton, in order

1. **Opener.** Role, company, years and stack in the same paragraph, plus a specific hook from the post.
2. **Lead project.** `The work I'd point you to first is ...` — what it is, who used it, the hard part,
   the judgement call. Four to six sentences.
3. **Supporting projects.** Two projects that answer the posting's requirements. `Before that, ...` works
   well; a third can arrive as `Earlier at {employer} ...`.
4. **Collaboration.** Code review as a habit, documentation for the next maintainer, explaining
   behaviour to non-engineers. Team scope and mentoring belong here.
5. **Honest gap** (only when needed). One short plain paragraph, then a pivot to what transfers.
6. **Links.** Portfolio and code, on their own line, from `config/identity.json`.
7. **Close.** Availability and notice period, matched to contract vs permanent.
8. **Sign-off.** `Best regards,` plus the copied contact block.

## Length

Aim for roughly 340–420 words. Anything much shorter reads rushed; anything much longer gets skimmed.

## Voice details worth copying

- First person, declarative, no adjectives about yourself. Confidence comes from naming what you built
  and the decision inside it, not from claiming quality.
- The judgement sentence is the signature move: a real trade-off or decision, written plainly.
- Trade-offs are stated as trade-offs, not as features.
- Sentence length varies hard. A long technical sentence is often followed by a short one.
- An occasional dry aside a person would actually write is fine.
- No "passionate", no "excited to", no "leverage", no "proven track record", no rule-of-three padding.

## Numbers: what is allowed

Structural counts are fine: years, module/page counts, team sizes, number of delivered projects.
Performance figures are not: no percentages, no latency, no uptime, no user counts, no "cut X by 40%".
The resume carries the numbers.

## Employer naming

Name past employers if that matches your own letters and your CV supports it. Keep project detail
concrete but never invented. If you prefer generic framing ("in a previous role"), set that rule in your
own `config/application-template.md`.

## Subject lines

Bare job title as posted is the safe default. `{Role} – {Your Name}` and `Application for {Role}
Position` are acceptable variants. Keep it short enough to read on a phone.

## Salutation

`Dear Recruiter,` by default. `Dear {Company} team,` when the application goes through a named agency
or consultancy.

## Notice period

- Contract role → `I'm available to start immediately on a contract.`
- Permanent role → `I'm available for an interview and can start with standard notice.`

## Mechanics

- Email body is the letter, nothing else. The resume PDF is the only required attachment.
- No hard line-wraps at 70 characters.
- Prefer zero em-dashes; it is a soft rule, not a hard one.
- Two different roles at the same company can receive two different letters the same day when the roles
  genuinely differ. Each gets its own opener and stack paragraph.
